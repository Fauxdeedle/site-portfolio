---
name: Universus
order: 3
category: product design
tagline: A full ecommerce redesign for a card game publisher — from checkout to homepage
description: >-
  A ground-up redesign of UVS Games' ecommerce and marketing sites — checkout
  flow, product pages, and two cohesive homepages for the publisher and its
  flagship card game.
client: UVS Games
role: UX/UI Design
year: "2024"
team: 2 designers, handoff to a 3rd-party dev team
heroImage: /images/universus-hero.png
problem: >-
  UVS Games was running a site built before they acquired the Universus card
  game IP. It no longer reflected the brand, made it hard to showcase new
  card sets, and offered no real path to purchase online.
goal: >-
  Design a full site experience that got players excited about new releases,
  made browsing and buying cards effortless, and gave UVS Games (the
  publisher) and Universus (the game) each a homepage that felt distinct but
  part of the same system.
process:
  - title: Checkout & cart flow
    description: >-
      Designed an express-checkout-first flow with a persistent slide-over
      cart, built to reduce drop-off and keep shoppers in context instead of
      navigating away from the product they were browsing.
  - title: Sales & product pages
    description: >-
      Designed the product detail and shop landing pages, including
      cross-sell modules and filterable browsing, then documented animation
      instructions for the dev team to implement on handoff.
keyDecisions:
  - title: Two homepages, one system
    description: >-
      UVS Games and Universus needed separate homepages (publisher vs.
      flagship game) without feeling like different brands, so both were
      built from the same visual language with different emphasis:
      product-forward for UVS Games, community/lore-forward for Universus.
finalDesigns:

  - /images/universus-final-2.png
  - /images/universus-final-3.png
  - /images/universus-final-4.png
result: >-
  Delivered full UX/UI designs — including the checkout flow, cart, product
  pages, and both homepages — with detailed animation specs, then handed off
  the full design system to a third-party development team in France for
  build.
